One set of panel toggles: the icon shows the layout, hover shows what a click will do

Today the same job is drawn four ways: double chevrons on the rail and the source control column, panel icons in Code, an X on the chat column, arrows on the browser. The proposal is one primitive: a small window outline whose shaded part is the panel. Shaded means open. It lives in one place, and every panel answers to it.

Read the layout from the icon
The outline is the window; the filled part is where the panel sits. Filled in text colour: that panel is on screen. Outline only, muted, with a divider where it would be: hidden.
Hover says what a click does
Hovering an open toggle fades its fill to a third (it is about to go). Hovering a closed one fades a fill in at half (it is about to come). The tooltip names the action and its key: "Hide source control ⌘B", "Show chat ⌥⌘B", "Show the bottom panel ⌘J".
Motion and access
160ms fade on the fill only; off under reduced motion. Each is a real button with aria-pressed and the same label as its tooltip, 28px hit target around a 20px drawing.
